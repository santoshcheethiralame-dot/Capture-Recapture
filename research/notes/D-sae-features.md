## 1. Summary (key takeaways)

1. **Cross-seed overlap of SAE latents is low and measured, so recapture rates are not near 1 and not near 0.** Paulo and Belrose (2501.16615) find that two SAEs differing only in init seed share roughly 42% of latents (Pythia-160M, MLP layer 6, 8B tokens, identical data order), and that a 131K-latent SAE on a Llama 3 8B feed-forward layer shares only about 30% across seeds (later version). That is the regime where Lincoln-Petersen and Chao have power: moderate overlap, K seeds cheap to produce.
2. **The "shared" fraction depends heavily on the matching rule and threshold**, which is the proposal's main risk (identity). Paulo-Belrose use Hungarian matching on decoder (and encoder) cosine; they report that Hungarian agrees mostly with simple max-cosine. Follow-ups use mean-max-cosine, fraction paired above 0.7 (Stable and Steerable SAEs, 2603.04198) and a stricter "shared" rule requiring the encoder and decoder matchings to agree. Pairwise MCC (Song et al., 2505.20254) is the assignment-based summary. We should always sweep the threshold and report the estimate as a function of it.
3. **Seed dependence is strongly heterogeneous across features.** "Unstable Features, Reproducible Subspaces" (2606.12138) measures per-feature stability: stable features carry most of the reconstruction/prediction signal, unstable ones are low-frequency, surface-form-triggered, and concentrated in reproducible low-rank subspaces (basis ambiguity). This is exactly the unequal-catchability setting of Chao's lower bound and Mh/Huggins models, and it says catchability correlates with feature frequency (a ready-made covariate).
4. **Data budget changes the recapture rate a lot**, so "occasion" quality is not fixed: Gemma-2-2B BatchTopK strict-matched share goes from 2% (5M tokens) to 29% (50M) (2610.20680). Reconstruction saturates long before reproducibility. Estimates must be indexed by training budget.
5. **TopK is less seed-stable than L1-ReLU at matched sparsity** (Paulo-Belrose abstract), yet Song et al. report PW-MCC around 0.80 for TopK with suitable design choices. Architecture is therefore a covariate on catchability and not just a nuisance.
6. **Different widths are nested, dependent catches.** Leask et al. (2502.04878) show smaller SAEs miss features present in larger ones (stitching) and that big-SAE latents decompose into small-SAE latents (meta-SAEs). Feature splitting (Bricken, Chanin absorption) makes the identity map one-to-many. Widths are not independent Bernoulli catches of the same unit. They are a hierarchy, and need either a collapse rule (many-to-one) or a model with known nesting.
7. **Sharkey, Braun and Millidge (2022) are the direct precursor.** In the toy setting, dictionary = number of ground-truth features and MMCS (mean over ground-truth vectors of max cosine to learned dictionary) measures recovery. For real LMs they compare dictionaries of different sizes using the smaller dictionary as the "ground truth" set. Replications found a bimodal distribution of max cosines across sizes, i.e. a consistent core. They never turned this into a population estimate with a variance, which is the gap we fill. (Seen via secondary descriptions only; I could not retrieve the post's text.)
8. **Synthetic ground truth now exists at scale.** SynthSAEBench (2602.14687): 16,384 true features, d=768, Zipfian firing probabilities, a 3-level mutually exclusive hierarchy over 10,884 features, a low-rank correlation matrix, recommended SAE width 4,096. So 12,288 true features are structurally out of reach of a 4,096-wide SAE. That makes it a ready Phase 1 testbed *and* a warning that "the population" must be defined as "features recoverable by this class of SAE", not all true features.
9. **Even in synthetic settings recovery is poor and not what loss suggests.** Korznikov et al. (2602.14111): about 9% of true features recovered at 71% explained variance. SynthSAEBench: best architecture (Matryoshka) reaches GT-F1 0.88 versus 0.97 for logistic regression, and Matching-Pursuit SAEs improve reconstruction by exploiting superposition noise. Reconstruction is a poor proxy for "caught", so use ground-truth-based truth to evaluate the estimators.
10. **Reseed noise has already been used as a diagnostic.** Chanin (2605.18229) audits SAEBench via reseed noise, synthetic ground-truth correlation and discriminability; TPP and SCR fail. The code is public (decoderesearch/saebench-reliability-audit), a ready source of multi-seed SAE runs and a synthetic-vs-real pipeline.
11. **No public suite offers many true replicates except EleutherAI/sparsify-style seed releases and what we train ourselves.** Gemma Scope, Gemma Scope 2 and Llama Scope vary width and L0 (nested/dependent), not seed. For replicate catches, plan to train our own small SAEs (Pythia-70M/160M, GPT-2) with 5 to 20 seeds. I could not confirm that EleutherAI released multi-seed checkpoints (the repos I saw, `sae-llama-3-8b-32x`, `sae-llama-3.1-8b-32x`, look like single-seed releases).
12. **Nobody appears to have estimated the total number of features in a model with a statistical estimator.** What exists: the quantization model (Zipfian quanta frequencies; exponent implies a power law with index about -1.083 from the measured alphaN=0.083), Scaling Monosemanticity's statement that the feature suite is incomplete and that the dictionary size needed to resolve a concept depends systematically on its frequency, and Gao et al.'s scaling laws in n latents. These supply priors (power-law frequency spectrum) for the estimators, but no census. I did not find a capture-recapture paper in this area in my searches (absence of evidence only, given the thin coverage).
13. **Dead latents and "dark matter" must be handled as part of the catch definition.** Gao et al.: only 7% dead latents in the 16M-latent TopK SAE with the auxiliary loss. Engels et al.: about half the error vector and over 90% of its norm is linearly predictable from the input, so a large part of the unexplained variance is not "missing features" but a predictable component. This means "features not found" is not equal to "residual variance", another reason to use ground truth to calibrate.
14. **Identifiability theory exists and gives conditions under which "same feature" is well defined**: Toward Identifiable SAEs (2605.31245) gives near-identifiability under low reconstruction error, dense manifold sampling, approximate RIP and diverse co-occurrence; its authors note the result does not certify ground-truth recovery. Taming Polysemanticity (2506.14002) and On the Limits of SAEs (2506.15963) also appeared (titles seen only).
15. **Practical design:** Phase 1 should use SynthSAEBench-style generators with heterogeneous frequency (Zipf) and magnitude, K=5 to 20 seeds per width, matching by Hungarian on decoder cosine with a threshold sweep (0.5 to 0.95) plus an activation-correlation check, and compare Chao1/Chao2/jackknife/Huggins-with-covariates to truth defined as "true features recoverable at this width and sample size". See section 3.

## 2. Papers

### 2.1 Consistency across runs

**Paulo and Belrose (2025), "Sparse Autoencoders Trained on the Same Data Learn Different Features", arXiv 2501.16615** (ICLR 2026 version at proceedings.iclr.cc). 
- Found (seen): SAEs with different seeds on the same data learn different features. Two SAEs on Pythia-160M MLP layer 6, 8B Pile tokens, same data order, differ only by init seed. Only about 42% of latents are shared. In a 131K-latent SAE on a Llama 3 8B feed-forward layer, only about 30% shared across seeds. Seen across several layers, three LLMs, two datasets, several architectures. ReLU+L1 SAEs are more stable across seeds than TopK, even at matched sparsity. Matching is Hungarian on encoder and decoder cosine; max-cosine matching mostly agrees. A shuffled-SAE baseline (permute encoder rows, inverse-permute decoder columns) is used as a control.
- Why it matters: it is the first quantitative "recapture" rate and the protocol we should copy (identical data order, only init varies, which is arguably *not* independent; see pitfalls). The 30% to 42% shared rate gives a rough expected Lincoln-Petersen result: if the true recoverable set were N and each seed caught n with m shared, N = n1*n2/m would be around 2 to 3 times n. We can test how this agrees with SynthSAEBench ground truth.
- Open: whether shared latents are more interpretable (I did not confirm); how the fraction varies with width; no population estimate.

**Leask et al. (2025), "Sparse Autoencoders Do Not Find Canonical Units of Analysis", arXiv 2502.04878** (ICLR 2025).
- Found (seen): SAE stitching shows that a larger SAE has novel latents the smaller one lacks (so smaller SAEs are incomplete). Meta-SAEs (SAE trained on a decoder matrix) show that latents are often combinations of smaller-SAE latents (non-atomic). Decompositions are often interpretable. I could not retrieve the numeric fractions.
- Why it matters: this is the evidence that widths are nested catches, and that "incomplete" is real. It gives two ready operations for the project: stitching = measure how many features of SAE B are absent from SAE A (a one-directional recapture count, cf. Lincoln-Petersen's m); meta-SAE = many-to-one collapse rule for splits.
- Open: a canonical unit; no counting.

**Song et al. (2025), "Position: Mechanistic Interpretability Should Prioritize Feature Consistency in SAEs", arXiv 2505.20254** (ACL 2026 long paper, aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.99).
- Found (seen): defines PW-MCC, the mean cosine of Hungarian-matched (max sum of |cos|) decoder columns of two independently trained dictionaries. Reaches about 0.80 for TopK SAEs on LLM activations with suitable architectural choices. In synthetic settings PW-MCC tracks ground-truth recovery; on real activations it correlates with similarity of automatically generated explanations of matched features. Theory for TopK.
- Why it matters: the most standard "same-feature" matching metric and a validation that cross-seed agreement tracks ground truth in toy settings, which is the premise of the whole project.
- Open: PW-MCC is an average, not a count above threshold; does not give a number of unmatched features.

**Gerasimov, Rusalev, Balagansky, Laptev, Kurochkin, Gavrilov (2026), "Unstable Features, Reproducible Subspaces: Understanding Seed Dependence in Sparse Autoencoders", arXiv 2606.12138.**
- Found (seen, abstract only): per-feature reappearance probability in an independently trained SAE, measured across seeds, models, layers, widths and SAE variants. Stable features carry most signal; unstable features have weak marginal effect and are often low-frequency surface-form triggers. They concentrate in reproducible low-rank subspaces. A synthetic setup shows low-rank ground truth recoverable at subspace level but not as individual latents. Pooling unique cross-seed features gives more stable SAEs.
- Why it matters: it is a direct empirical statement of heterogeneous catchability and that catchability correlates with frequency. It also suggests a subspace-level definition of identity as a robustness variant. "Pooling unique cross-seed features" is exactly a union-of-catches, whose size is what we estimate.

**Ghosh, Mathai, Babu, Agarwal (2026), "Diminishing Returns of Data in Sparse Autoencoders: Functional Behavior does not Guarantee Feature Reproducibility", arXiv 2610.20680.**
- Found (seen): four architectures, three LMs, budgets from 5M to 500M tokens. Reconstruction converges at small budgets, feature reproducibility keeps rising. Gemma-2-2B BatchTopK strictly matched share rises from 2% (5M) to 29% (50M).
- Why it matters: catchability depends on training budget; gives a natural covariate/experimental axis; warns that "estimates at budget B" is the estimand.

**Song-style follow-ups and critiques seen by title/abstract only:**
- "Stable and Steerable Sparse Autoencoders with Weight Regularization", arXiv 2603.04198 (ICML 2026 MI workshop). Small L2 weight penalty on TopK SAEs (Pythia-70M-deduped; MNIST) raises the fraction of features shared across three seeds; with tied init and unit-norm decoder it improves cross-seed consistency; steering success about doubles. Reports mean max cosine, fraction paired above 0.7, and a stricter shared criterion (encoder and decoder matchings agree). Use: a *manipulable catchability* knob, and its metrics are the matching rules to sweep. (Exact numbers not seen.)
- "Ensembling Sparse Autoencoders", arXiv 2505.16077. Bagging (different init seeds) and boosting (residual) ensembles improve reconstruction, feature diversity and stability; bagging has redundancy because SAEs with different init still learn overlapping features. Use: the diversity of bagged union is literally the union-of-catches the estimators extrapolate; one can test "estimated population vs size of ensemble union" as sanity check.
- "Ablating Archetypes: The Stability of Archetypal SAEs is an Artifact of Initialization and Metric Design", arXiv 2606.02061, critiquing Fel et al. (2025) "Archetypal SAE", arXiv 2502.12892 (decoder atoms constrained to the convex hull of data; RA-SAE relaxed variant). Critique: archetypal stability comes from shared decoder init and from cosine similarity being deflated when atoms sit far from origin. Pitfall for us: **shared initialisation produces artificial recaptures**, and cosine-based matching depends on centering.
- "Aligned Training: A Parameter-Free Method to Improve Feature Quality and Stability of SAEs", arXiv 2605.18629 (title only). "C^2R: Cross-sample Consistency Regularization Mitigates Feature Splitting and Absorption", arXiv 2606.30609 (title only). "Beyond SAEs: Consistent Concept Discovery with Sparse Coding", arXiv 2610.23801 (title only). "OrtSAE", arXiv 2509.22033 (title only). All are methods that change catchability; not read.
- "From Geometric Recovery to Causal Validation: A Reproducible Audit of SAE Features", arXiv 2607.12166 (title only; might contain reproducible ground-truth recoveries).

**Lan et al. (2024), "Sparse Autoencoders Reveal Universal Feature Spaces Across Large Language Models" (retitled "Quantifying Feature Space Universality Across LLMs via SAEs"), arXiv 2410.06981.**
- Found (seen): train SAEs on different LLMs, match feature neurons across models by **activation correlation** (to solve neuron permutation), then compare with SVCCA-type metrics (to handle rotation). Finds substantial similarity of SAE feature spaces across LLMs. Whether Hungarian is used was not confirmed.
- Why it matters: the only major precedent for activation-correlation matching across independently trained SAEs on *different* models. This is the right method for architectures/models where decoder spaces differ, and for a second matching rule beside decoder cosine.

**Bricken et al. (2023), "Towards Monosemanticity" (Anthropic).** Not searched, not seen. From memory (unverified): features in 1-layer transformers are similar across seeds of the model and SAE (quantified by correlation of activations) and split into finer features as dictionary size grows ("feature splitting"). Needs checking before citing. Search should be redone when the budget returns.

**Sharkey, Braun, Millidge (2022), "[Interim research report] Taking features out of superposition with sparse autoencoders", LessWrong/Conjecture.** (Seen via listings and secondary descriptions; the post text itself not retrieved.)
- Found: sparse coding with L1 on a single-layer autoencoder recovers the *ground-truth* features of a toy dataset and not just a loss-minimiser; then applied to MLP activations of a ~31M-parameter LM they trained. Recovery measured by MMCS. For LMs there is no ground truth, so dictionaries of different sizes are compared with the smaller set playing ground truth. Later notes: the "diagonals" in the original figure come from the L1 coefficient being divided by dictionary size; replication (AF post "[Replication] Conjecture's Sparse Coding in Small Transformers") found a bimodal max-cosine distribution across sizes (a stable core plus a tail); another study found average cosine decreasing as latents increase.
- Why it matters: this is the closest antecedent. They used agreement across dictionary sizes as evidence about the number of features, with no probabilistic model. Our contribution: replace MMCS by an explicit capture model, include the nesting, and validate against known truth. Careful reading target: how they chose the "number of features" from the MMCS-vs-size curve (a plateau?). Unverified here. Also note MMCS is asymmetric (max over the smaller set), which is not a capture count.
- Follow-up seen: "Taking features out of superposition with sparse autoencoders more quickly with informed initialization" (LessWrong): initialization effects on recovery (not read).

### 2.2 Architectures and structure that change catchability

- **Bussmann, Nabeshima, Karvonen, Nanda (2025), "Learning Multi-Level Features with Matryoshka Sparse Autoencoders", arXiv 2503.17547 (ICML 2025).** Seen: nested dictionaries of increasing size trained at once, early latents must reconstruct alone, so they capture general features. Gemma-2-2B and TinyStories. Reduces feature absorption, more disentangled (by max decoder cosine), better sparse probing and concept erasure, small reconstruction loss. Why it matters: an architecture with *built-in* nesting, i.e. the widths-as-occasions with known nesting case is structurally exact for it. Also best architecture in SynthSAEBench. Nabeshima's separate Matryoshka write-up: not seen.
- **Chanin et al. (2024), "A is for Absorption: Studying Feature Splitting and Absorption in SAEs", arXiv 2409.14507 (NeurIPS 2025).** Seen: absorption = a latent for a general feature fails to fire where a more specific latent covers it; measured on first-letter tasks in Gemma-2-2B (and Gemma Scope 16k and 65k SAEs), rate = absorptions / probe true positives. Hyperparameter tuning does not fully fix it. Why it matters: absorption means a feature can be "caught" yet its catch is partial/ambiguous, and an undercounting source when matching by decoder cosine alone (absorbed latent has a hole in its firing pattern).
- **Chanin, Dulka, Garriga-Alonso (2025), "Feature Hedging: Correlated Features Break Narrow SAEs", arXiv 2505.11756.** Seen: when the SAE is narrower than the number of true features, correlated features are merged into latents (hedging), worse the narrower the SAE; needs only correlation, not hierarchy; proposes an improved matryoshka. Why: tells us that for width < true count the "catch" is a *mixture* so decoder cosine to any true feature falls below a threshold: the narrow-width regime systematically underestimates catches and violates the clear-identity assumption.
- **Chanin et al. (2025), "Sparse but Wrong: Incorrect L0 Leads to Incorrect Features in SAEs", arXiv 2508.16560.** Seen only as a title/snippet (shows SAEs mix correlated features if L0 is wrong; explores hedging). Use: L0 relative to the true L0 is a nuisance axis for Phase 1 (include "L0 equals true L0" and "L0 too low/high" cells).
- **Balagansky, Maksimov, Gavrilov (2024), "Mechanistic Permutability: Match Features Across Layers" (SAE Match), arXiv 2410.07656 (ICLR 2025).** Seen: data-free matching of SAE features between layers by solving a linear assignment problem between decoder columns after "folding" JumpReLU thresholds into the weights, minimizing MSE; many features in mid-to-late layers have near one-to-one matches with preceding layers; Gemma 2. Use: (a) folding thresholds is the right normalisation when comparing JumpReLU SAEs (Gemma Scope) where per-feature scale lives in the threshold; (b) a data-free matching alternative for Phase 2; (c) adjacent layers are another source of "recaptures" (a feature persisting across layers is not a seed replicate though). Same group wrote 2606.12138.
- **Engels, Riggs, Tegmark (2024), "Decomposing the Dark Matter of Sparse Autoencoders", arXiv 2410.14670.** Seen: about half of the SAE error vector and >90% of its norm is linearly predictable from the input; the nonlinear remainder contains fewer not-yet-learned features and accounts for a proportional share of downstream loss increase; error norm predictability holds at all widths and L0, vector predictability falls with width and L0. Use: bounds on how much of the unexplained variance could even correspond to missing features, and an independent view of "missed" content to compare with the capture-recapture estimate.
- **Templeton et al. (2024), "Scaling Monosemanticity: Extracting Interpretable Features from Claude 3 Sonnet" (Anthropic; transformer-circuits.pub/2024/scaling-monosemanticity).** Seen: SAEs up to 34M features on the mid-layer residual stream; features are multilingual and multimodal; **systematic relationship between concept frequency and the dictionary size needed to resolve a feature**; authors state their suite of features is incomplete. An arXiv listing with the same title (2605.29358) also appeared; I could not tell whether it is a repost, so cite transformer-circuits.pub unless verified. I did not retrieve their quoted statement about estimating how many features exist (my memory is that they estimate that finding all features would require far more compute than training the model, unverified). Use: the frequency-vs-resolution relation is a ready covariate for catchability, and the incompleteness statement is the claim we aim to quantify.
- **Gao et al. (2024), "Scaling and evaluating sparse autoencoders", arXiv 2406.04093 (ICLR 2025).** Seen: TopK SAE, 16M latents on GPT-4 for 40B tokens, k=32; only 7% dead latents with the auxiliary loss; clean joint scaling laws in n latents and k; feature-quality metrics improve with size. Use: scaling law in width is an alternative extrapolation (loss versus n) to compare against capture-recapture; dead-latent fraction is a catch-model nuisance.
- **Michaud et al. (2023), "The Quantization Model of Neural Scaling", arXiv 2303.13506 (NeurIPS 2023).** Seen: network learns discrete "quanta" in order of use frequency; Zipfian use-frequency distribution yields power-law scaling; measured loss exponent alphaN = 0.083 implies a power law over quanta with index about -1.083 (so frequency of k-th quantum ~ k^-(1+alpha), alpha about 0.083). Use: the frequency spectrum that the Chao/jackknife estimators have to cope with is plausibly extremely heavy-tailed (alpha near 0, a very long tail of rare features). This predicts that the singleton count f1 is huge and Chao1 diverges or has a huge variance. Real concern: if the tail is like Zipf with exponent near 1, the number of species is effectively unbounded and "total features" is ill-defined, only "features above frequency f" is. Phase 2 should therefore report N(f_min) for a frequency cutoff.
- **Brill (2024), "Neural Scaling Laws Rooted in the Data Distribution", arXiv 2412.07942, and "AlphaZero Neural Scaling and Zipf's Law", arXiv 2412.11979, and "Understanding sparse autoencoder scaling in the presence of feature manifolds", arXiv 2509.02565** (all seen by title/snippet only). Related to how scaling and Zipf apply; not read.

### 2.4 Synthetic ground truth

**SynthSAEBench: Evaluating Sparse Autoencoders on Scalable Realistic Synthetic Data, arXiv 2602.14687** (Chanin and collaborators, 2026; first-author attribution unverified; emerging "decoderesearch" org).
- Seen: SynthSAEBench-16k: 16,384 ground-truth features, hidden dim 768 (superposition about 0.15?, as quoted), Zipfian firing probabilities, 3-level mutually exclusive hierarchy across 10,884 features, random low-rank correlation matrix. Metrics GT-MCC (match SAE decoder columns to true directions by |cos| and average) and GT-F1 (match each latent to best-correlated true feature, F1 of firing). Recommended SAE width 4,096 ("in-SAE" first 4,096 features, "out-of-SAE" remaining 12,288). Best architecture (Matryoshka) F1 0.88 vs 0.97 for a logistic regression probe; Matching Pursuit SAEs exploit superposition noise.
- Why it matters: **this is the Phase 1 testbed**, with ground truth and realistic heterogeneity already built in; we can regenerate with our own parameters. The in-SAE/out-of-SAE split is a built-in "population that cannot be caught" and tests whether estimators estimate the recoverable or the total population.

**Korznikov et al. (2026), "Sanity Checks for Sparse Autoencoders: Do SAEs Beat Random Baselines?", arXiv 2602.14111.**
- Seen: SAEs recover about 9% of true features at 71% explained variance in synthetic data with known features; three frozen/random baselines (fixed random directions or activation patterns) match trained SAEs on interpretability (0.87 vs 0.90), sparse probing (0.69 vs 0.72), causal editing (0.73 vs 0.72). Feature count and dimensions not seen.
- Why: if only about 9% of true features are recovered, the catch probability is low and the Lincoln-Petersen estimate is likely to be badly biased by heterogeneity (a few easy features caught every time, most never caught); good stress test. Also, the *random-baseline* result matters: a "frozen random" SAE is a degenerate catch process, a null for our matching (random dictionaries produce spurious matches at a threshold; use as false-positive calibration of the match rule).

**Chanin (2026), "Are Sparse Autoencoder Benchmarks Reliable?", arXiv 2605.18229.**
- Seen: three lenses: reseed noise of a fixed SAE, ground-truth correlation with synthetic SAEs, discriminability across training trajectories. TPP and SCR fail at canonical settings; sparse probing, sae-probes, autointerp and RAVEL-disentangle have low reseed variance; sae-probes is the best metric but struggles to separate variants of one architecture. ## 3. Leads, inspirations and experiment ideas

### Phase 1: synthetic testbed design
1. **Generator.** Start from SynthSAEBench's open generator (or Elhage-style toy model) and set: N_true in {1k, 4k, 16k}; model dim d chosen for target superposition; **firing probabilities p_i ~ Zipf (rank-frequency exponent 0.5 to 1.5, or lognormal)** to mimic quantization-model spectra (Michaud alpha about 0.08 implies an extremely flat-then-heavy tail; sweep wide), with a floor so that every feature fires in the training sample at least a few times (otherwise "unobservable" features appear that no estimator can see). **Magnitudes** drawn from lognormal/gamma, independent of p in one arm and *correlated with p* in another (because Gemma-style JumpReLU thresholds/norm interact with frequency). Include arms with hierarchy and correlation (absorption/hedging, per 2409.14507/2505.11756) and orthogonal (no correlation) arms. Add noise to activations.
2. **Define the target estimand explicitly.** Three nested truths: (a) N_true; (b) N_obs(T) = features that fired at least c times in training sample of T tokens; (c) N_rec(arch, width) = features *recoverable* by an oracle SAE of that class (e.g. features with GT-MCC above threshold in the best of 50 seeds, or in a "super-union" ensemble). Report estimates against (b) and (c), and show how far estimates are from (a). SynthSAEBench's in/out-of-SAE split (4,096 vs 12,288) is the stress test: a width-4,096 SAE can never catch more than 4,096 features, so Chao1 should estimate something near the recoverable count, if anything.
3. **Dictionary sizes.** Widths W in {0.25, 0.5, 1, 2, 4}x N_true (under- and over-complete; hedging below, splitting above). For each width, K = 10 to 20 seeds (init-only, data-order-only, both) because Chao-type estimators need enough occasions to see f1 and f2 (K=2 gives only Lincoln-Petersen). Also vary L0 relative to the true L0 (0.5x, 1x, 2x; "Sparse but Wrong"), architecture (ReLU/L1, TopK, BatchTopK, JumpReLU, Matryoshka), and training tokens T (diminishing-returns paper: reproducibility rises with data).
4. **Matching.** Primary: Hungarian one-to-one matching on |decoder cosine| (Song's PW-MCC logic; Paulo-Belrose), applied to pairs of runs, then identity chain across K runs by connected components of the thresholded match graph (or by matching each run to a *reference* run, which biases; better: all-pairs graph with clique/cluster constraint). Secondary: activation correlation of latents on shared tokens (Lan et al.), "strict" rule requiring encoder and decoder matchings to agree (2603.04198), and mutual nearest neighbour as a conservative rule. Thresholds: sweep 0.5 to 0.95. For JumpReLU SAEs, fold thresholds before comparing scales (SAE Match). Calibrate false matches with a shuffled/random-dictionary null (random vectors in d=768 have |cos| tiny, but random vs trained-on-same-data dictionaries are not tiny: Korznikov's frozen random baselines).
5. **Truth-aware matching experiment.** Because truth is known, compute *two* capture histories for each SAE: (i) matched-to-ground-truth (the oracle history) and (ii) matched-across-seeds (the history estimators see). Differences between estimators on (i) vs (ii) isolate the cost of imperfect identity matching from the cost of heterogeneity. This is the cleanest decomposition of error sources and is cheap on synthetic data.
6. **Estimators to run** (Rcapture or own Python): Lincoln-Petersen/Chapman for pairs, Chao1 (abundance-based) and Chao2 (incidence-based, the right one here: each seed is a sampling unit and each feature is detected or not), first/second-order jackknife, ICE, Good-Turing sample coverage per seed, log-linear Mt/Mh/Mth, Huggins conditional-likelihood with covariates (R: VGAM `posbernoulli.*` families or Rcapture closedpCI; unverified function names from memory). Also a Poisson/lognormal mixture and a Pitman-Yor / Zipf-tail extrapolation for the heavy-tail case.
7. **Covariates in Huggins.** For each caught feature we can compute before matching: **activation frequency, mean/median activation magnitude, decoder norm (JumpReLU threshold), maximum cosine to its nearest neighbour in the same dictionary (a splitting/absorption indicator), position in the Matryoshka prefix, and the pair's aRIP-like local coherence**. Problem: Huggins conditions on being caught, so covariates must be known for caught individuals only; fine here (unobserved ones just do not enter the likelihood). Test "frequency-only", "frequency + magnitude", and "add coherence". Gerasimov et al. say stable features are frequent and surface-form-triggered, so frequency should be strongly predictive; the interesting question is whether a logit-linear frequency effect extrapolates to the rare tail (it can only if the relationship is smooth and the tail has support).
8. **Widths as occasions with known nesting.** Treat the width ladder (Gemma Scope-style 16k, 65k, 131k, ..., or synthetic W_j) as ordered occasions in which catchability p_j increases with width. A model: logit p_ij = a_j + b*x_i (Huggins with time effects, Mt) plus a *nesting constraint* that a feature caught at width W is expected to be caught (or split) at W' > W (monotone detection, as in removal/"depletion" designs or as a cumulative-link / accumulation curve). Compare with species-accumulation / rarefaction extrapolation (Colwell-style, unverified) on the number of distinct matched features vs width. In the synthetic setting, test whether the width ladder predicts the seed-replicate estimate.
9. **Estimating via split-half** (sampling-theory validation): subsample the K seeds and check coverage and bias of CIs (bootstrap over seeds and over features) against truth, as a function of K, W/N_true, frequency spectrum, matching threshold. Expect: Chao1/Chao2 as lower bounds that are too low for heavy-tailed spectra; jackknife less biased but wider; Lincoln-Petersen biased low under positive dependence between runs (shared data, shared easy features). The "shared data order" design of Paulo-Belrose induces positive dependence: add arms where each seed uses an independent data stream.
10. **Subspace-level identity as a robustness variant** (2606.12138): define an individual as a unit in a low-rank cluster of decoder directions; check whether estimates stabilise (a version of the "coarse-graining invariance" test).
11. **Negative/positive controls:** (a) random-dictionary SAEs (frozen random baselines) should give ~zero recapture beyond the null; (b) a deliberately easy toy (orthogonal features, W=N_true, high T) should yield near-perfect Lincoln-Petersen; (c) injected "super-rare" features (p about 1e-4) the SAE can never learn at the given T, to show the "never catchable" mass is not estimable (identifiability limit of any capture-recapture estimator under heterogeneity, as in Link 2003 on non-identifiability of N under heterogeneity, unverified).

### Phase 2: real-model design
1. **Replicates (independent catches).** Train K = 5 to 10 seeds myself at modest scale: Pythia-70M/160M (Paulo-Belrose protocol, `sparsify`/SAELens), or Gemma-2-2B 16k BatchTopK at 50M to 500M tokens (diminishing-returns paper shows 29% strict match at 50M tokens, so budget matters). Report estimates vs training tokens. Include ReLU/L1 and TopK arms (TopK less stable).
2. **Nested ladder (dependent catches).** Gemma Scope Gemma-2-2B (or 9B) `resid_post` at a mid layer, widths 16k, 65k, 131k, 262k(524k), 1M, matched L0 (e.g. medium ~70); and Gemma Scope 2 4B/12B at the 4 deep layers with widths 16k/64k/256k/1M and L0 small/medium/big. Use the ladder as occasions-with-nesting (idea 8) and L0 variants at a fixed width as "near-replicates" (check how correlated they are). Estimated N(W) should flatten with W if the feature population is finite and rise linearly if not; a diagnostic for the Zipf-tail concern.
3. **Cross-lab catches**: Gemma-2-2B has Gemma Scope (JumpReLU, DeepMind), SAEBench/other architectures from the community, chanind's BatchTopK Matryoshka 32k, and Eleuther-style TopK; different architectures and training data are close to "independent investigators" (heterogeneous catchability by design, Mt/Mh). Match by activation correlation on a common token set (Lan et al.), since decoder spaces are in the same residual basis in this case (so cosine also works).
4. **Sampling-frame warning:** report estimates for "features with firing frequency above f_min on distribution D", not "all features", since Zipf-like tails make N unbounded (Michaud).
5. **Compare to independent heuristics**: Gao's scaling law extrapolation; Engels' dark-matter fraction (predictable part is not missing features); Templeton's frequency-resolution rule; meta-SAE/stitching counts (Leask).
6. **Reuse existing code**: Paulo-Belrose matching code (EleutherAI GitHub; location unverified), Chanin's `saebench-reliability-audit` and SynthSAEBench repos (decoderesearch org), SAE Match code (Balagansky/T-Tech).

### Other ideas
- **Pooled union as an "oracle" proxy.** Union of K seeds (as in Ensembling SAEs and 2606.12138's pooling) approaches the recoverable population as K grows; use the K-curve (accumulation curve) as a direct empirical check of what Chao predicts.
- **Per-seed coverage** (Good-Turing: fraction of activation mass explained by features also found in other seeds) gives a coverage statistic in "mass" terms, which is more meaningful than count terms for safety (a missed feature that is rare costs little). Combine with Engels' error decomposition.
- **Safety-relevant strata:** estimate the unseen population separately for feature classes (e.g. deception/refusal-related, found via Neuronpedia labels or probes), to answer "how many safety-relevant features did we miss", the headline application.
- **Cross-layer persistence** (SAE Match) as a second occasion axis: a feature can be "recaptured" in an adjacent layer; useful for heterogeneity but not independent.

## 4. Pitfalls and warnings

1. **Closed-population assumption vs width.** The set of features a width-W SAE can represent is bounded by W and is subject to hedging below N_true and splitting above N_true. The estimand drifts with W. Define it explicitly (recoverable under architecture and budget) and never compare estimates across widths as if they were the same N.
2. **Nested widths are not independent occasions.** A feature split in a larger SAE yields several "recaptures" of one parent (inflating m or creating many-to-one), and a small SAE's merged latent matches none cleanly (hedging, absorption). Requires a collapse rule (meta-SAE style or many-to-one assignment) or a model with known nesting.
3. **Shared design induces positive dependence.** Same data order (Paulo-Belrose), same init (Archetypal SAE critique), same base model/activations, and the same tokens in training mean runs are not independent, so Lincoln-Petersen is biased low (N underestimated) and Chao lower bounds are even more conservative. Vary data order independently from init and test.
4. **Heterogeneity is strong and frequency-linked**: stable features carry most signal; unstable ones are rare, surface-form triggers (2606.12138). Chao1 is only a lower bound; with Zipf-like tails the lower bound can be far from the truth, and the singletons are mostly *not* "almost caught" but genuinely hard.
5. **Matching threshold drives everything**; "shared" rates differ with encoder-vs-decoder rule, Hungarian vs max-cosine, threshold (0.7 is a common choice but arbitrary). Cosine can be inflated or deflated by offset/centering (Ablating Archetypes). Always give estimate-vs-threshold curves and use a null (shuffled/random dictionary).
6. **Reconstruction ≠ recovery.** 71% explained variance but 9% recovery (2602.14111); Matching Pursuit SAEs exploit superposition noise (SynthSAEBench). Do not use loss to decide who "caught" what.
7. **Synthetic realism**: results on SynthSAEBench-style data may not transfer (real activations have non-Gaussian structure, feature manifolds, dark matter); frequency spectra are assumed. State this and include misspecified-generator arms (e.g. generate from one family, fit another).
8. **Dead latents** and duplicate latents inside one SAE: they inflate n_i without catches, and duplicates within a run can match one feature twice. Deduplicate within run before matching.
9. **Scaling Monosemanticity citation oddity**: an arXiv ID 2605.29358 appeared with the same title; verify versions before citing numbers.
10. **Data budget confound**: reproducibility is strongly budget-dependent (2% to 29% on Gemma-2-2B). Report estimates per training budget; do not pool SAEs of different budgets as replicates.
11. **Heavy tails make N ill-defined.** If the true spectrum is close to Zipf(1), N diverges; report N(f_min) and rarefaction/extrapolation curves instead of a single total.
12. **Gemma Scope has no seeds.** Using its widths and L0 values as "occasions" yields dependent, ordered catches. Say so in any paper; do not present them as replicates.
13. **My coverage gaps** (above): Elhage, Bricken, Karvonen, InterpBench, Heap, identifiability literature were not searched; no paper was read in full text; several numeric claims are second-hand.
