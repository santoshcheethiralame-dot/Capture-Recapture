Written 22 Oct 2026. Companion to `D_sae_features.md` (not repeated here). Budget used: 43 of 45 WebSearch calls. WebFetch and curl are blocked (DNS), so no paper was read in full; "seen" means it appeared in a search result or in the search tool's summary of the page. ## 1. Summary

1. **SynthSAEBench ships with multi-seed SAEs and ground truth, so Phase 1 can start without training anything.** The HF repo `decoderesearch/synth-sae-bench-16k-v1-saes` holds SAEs of 5 architectures, each with **5 seeds** and L0 from 15 to 45 (7 L0 values: 15, 20, ..., 45, per Chanin 2605.18229), width 4,096, trained on 200M samples. That is 5 architectures x 7 L0 x 5 seeds = 175 SAEs (my arithmetic from the seen facts; the repo's exact file count is unverified). Ground truth: 16,384 unit-norm features in d=768. This is the ideal "replicates with known truth" set. A second collection, `chanind-goodfire/synthsaebench-full-width-sweep`, has 209 SAEs with 16,384 latents (width equal to N_true), 200M samples (seed structure unverified; its scores are explicitly not comparable with the 4,096-wide board).
2. **Generator details confirmed (section 2.1):** 16,384 features; d=768 (superposition ratio about 0.15); Zipfian firing probabilities p_i proportional to i^-alpha rescaled into [p_min, p_max] (alpha, p_min, p_max NOT found); firing indicators from a **Gaussian copula** with a random low-rank correlation matrix (rank r=25, scale s=0.1); magnitudes c_i = z_i * ReLU(mu_i + sigma_i * eps) (rectified Gaussian, per-feature mean and std; one variant card says "random std per feature with mean 0.5"); hierarchy of **128 disjoint trees, branching factor 4, depth 3, 10,880 nodes** (model card and paper; a secondary review says 10,884, treat 10,880 as correct), children mutually exclusive and only active when parent is active; remaining 5,504 features non-hierarchical. v1 world was uploaded with `scale_children_by_parent=False` by mistake; **v2** (`decoderesearch/synth-sae-bench-16k-v2`) fixes this and is otherwise identical.
3. **Authors:** David Chanin and Adria Garriga-Alonso (arXiv 2602.14687, Feb 2026, v2 Jul 2026, ICML 2026 listing). This settles the "first-author unverified" flag in D. Code: `github.com/decoderesearch/synth-sae-bench-experiments`; generator is inside SAELens (`github.com/decoderesearch/SAELens`).
4. **Recovery metrics (confirmed definitions):** GT-MCC = Hungarian assignment of SAE decoder columns to ground-truth directions by |cosine|, then mean; GT-F1 = each latent matched to its best-correlated true feature, then F1 of latent firing vs true firing. Chanin's audit adds an oracle SAE (decoder = first 4,096 true directions) with GT-MCC = GT-F1 = 1 by construction, and notes the MCC ceiling with a perfect encoder at width 4,096 is about 0.87 (secondary write-up, unverified). Supervised logistic-regression probes on the 4,096 most frequent features get mean F1 about 0.974.
5. **Sharkey et al. (2022) did not estimate the number of features from MMCS; they proposed three heuristics and said the answer was non-obvious.** What actually worked was toy-model recovery with learned dictionary between 1x and about 8x the true count (MMCS plateau). The Anthropic May 2023 Circuits Update adds the only "count" signal I found: on synthetic sparse data randomly projected to 32 dims, solutions "bounce" when dictionary size equals the true number of factors, and the **rate-distortion Pareto frontier** is occupied by solutions with the correct number of factors, where MMCS peaks. That is a model-selection heuristic, not an estimator with uncertainty, and only shown on synthetic data.
6. **I found no paper that uses capture-recapture, Chao or any species-richness estimator on SAE features** (one extended search, plus the D report's searches). The novelty claim stands, with the usual caveat about absence of evidence. Related: an OthelloGPT study found SAEs recover only 9/180 (later 33/180) probe-defined board features, and Karvonen et al. report coverage 0.48 (chess) and 0.52 (Othello) for trained SAEs, i.e. "found fraction" with known truth is far from 1 in real models.
7. **Identifiability theory gives the formal limit on what fraction can be caught:** Cui et al. (2506.15963, ICLR 2026) prove SAEs generally cannot recover ground-truth features unless the true features are extremely sparse (conditions: extremely sparse ground truth, sparse SAE activation, enough hidden dimensions), and show **feature shrinking and feature vanishing** failure modes. Tang et al. (2512.05534) show the SAE loss is piecewise biconvex with non-identifiability sources and spurious local minima. Together: the "recoverable population" is a property of (data, architecture, sparsity), and non-recovery is partly a deterministic optimisation property, not just sampling noise. This matters for the independence assumption.
8. **Frequency spectra in real SAEs:** the one quantitative fit seen (Empirical Evaluation of Progressive Coding for SAEs, 2505.00190): TopK SAEs (16k, 32k, 65k) have a central-region power law with exponent **-1.00 to -1.20** (R^2 0.868 to 0.887) and a **tail that falls faster than the power law** (rarest latents fire less than predicted). Gao et al. found log-density of latents roughly Gaussian (log-normal densities) with no multiple modes. Another paper (unnamed in my result, the one analysing Gao et al.'s open-source SAEs) reports a Zipf fit with R^2=0.982 (unverified attribution). Dense latents (fire on 10 to 50% of tokens) are real and form antipodal pairs (Sun et al., 2506.15679).
9. **Replicate-seed SAEs on real LMs are still not public as far as I can find.** The only confirmed real-LM replicates: Chanin's reseed audit (but that reseeds the *evaluation* of one fixed Gemma Scope 65k SAE, 5 times, not SAE training); 2606.12138 trains 96 seeds for its geometric analysis (release unverified). I could not confirm any release of Paulo-Belrose, Gerasimov or Ghosh seed checkpoints. Training our own is required for Phase 2.
10. **2610.19636 is not an SAE-run matching method.** It is SAE-IDF: using Gemma Scope/Llama Scope 131k-feature codes as a shared coordinate system to match symbols across heterogeneous systems, with rarity (IDF) re-weighting. The idea worth borrowing is rarity weighting; it does not help match features across SAE runs. The relevant matching paper is 2605.28567 (semantic optimal transport), which represents each feature by its activation-weighted distribution over hidden states (Wasserstein distance), claims invariance to rescaling, and beats decoder-vector baselines (numbers not seen).

## 2. Papers and sources

### 2.1 SynthSAEBench (priority 1)

**Chanin and Garriga-Alonso (2026), "SynthSAEBench: Evaluating Sparse Autoencoders on Scalable Realistic Synthetic Data", arXiv 2602.14687.** (https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14687, HTML https://arxiv.org/html/2602.14687v2.) ICML 2026 listing; Decode Research, MATS.

Confirmed facts (each from at least one result):
- **Toolkit** inside SAELens generating large-scale synthetic data with correlation, hierarchy and superposition, with ground-truth directions and firings. Fig. 1 caption says it scales to hundreds of thousands of features and realistic hidden dims.
- **Benchmark model SynthSAEBench-16k:** N=16,384 random unit-norm feature directions, D=768, superposition rho about 0.15. SAE input = sparse linear combination of unit-norm directions plus a bias.
- **Firing probabilities:** options Zipfian (p_i proportional to i^-alpha, rescaled into [p_min, p_max]), linear decay, uniform random, constant. The benchmark uses Zipfian; feature 0 is the most frequent. **alpha, p_min, p_max not found.** Average L0 is not stated in what I saw; the SAEs are trained at target L0 15 to 45 (about 25 is the middle value, so the true mean L0 is probably near 25, but this is an inference).
- **Correlation:** firing indicators drawn through a Gaussian copula: each feature's threshold = inverse normal CDF of (1 - p_i); fires when a correlated Gaussian sample exceeds it; with no correlation this reduces to independent Bernoulli(p_i). Correlation matrix is random low-rank (r=25, scale s=0.1; from the HF card "Feature correlation: Yes (scale 0.1)" and a review).
- **Hierarchy:** 128 roots, 10,880 hierarchy nodes in total, max depth 3, branching 4; child fires only if parent fires; siblings mutually exclusive; option `scale_children_by_parent` for magnitudes (the v1 upload had it False in error; v2 fixed). Non-hierarchical remainder: 5,504 features.
- **Magnitudes:** c_i = z_i ReLU(mu_i + sigma_i eps), eps ~ N(0,1); per-feature mu_i and sigma_i. A variation card mentions random per-feature std with mean 0.5 (secondary). Not found: the distribution of mu_i and sigma_i.
- **Standard evaluation:** SAE width 4,096, 200M training samples; "in-SAE" = first 4,096 features (the most frequent under Zipf, because the index orders probability), "out-of-SAE" = remaining 12,288.
- **SAE types:** standard L1, Matching Pursuit, BatchTopK, Matryoshka BatchTopK, JumpReLU.
- **Findings:** Matching-Pursuit SAEs get the best reconstruction but poor GT-MCC and F1 (they exploit superposition noise); Matryoshka gets the best MCC and F1 with poor explained variance (from secondary summary); best F1 about 0.88 vs 0.97 for probes (from D). The abstract also says the benchmark reproduces known LLM-SAE behaviour (feature splitting/absorption/hedging style failures).
- **Released artefacts:**
  - Generator model v2: https://huggingface.co/decoderesearch/synth-sae-bench-16k-v2 (v1 at `decoderesearch/synth-sae-bench-16k-v1`)
  - SAE zoo: https://huggingface.co/decoderesearch/synth-sae-bench-16k-v1-saes (5 types x 5 seeds x L0 15 to 45, each with a stats.json of eval results)
  - Full-width sweep: https://huggingface.co/chanind-goodfire/synthsaebench-full-width-sweep (209 SAEs, 16,384 latents)
  - Variants: https://huggingface.co/chanind/synth-sae-bench-variations (only the title was seen; contents unverified)
  - Experiments: https://github.com/decoderesearch/synth-sae-bench-experiments
  - A Claude auto-research task built on it: https://github.com/chanind/claude-auto-research-synthsaebench (TASK.md) and the LessWrong post "Letting Claude do Autonomous Research to Improve SAEs" (mentions SynthSAEBench use, not read).
  - PR #753 in SAELens documents the v1/v2 issue.

Why it matters / how to use it:
- With 5 seeds per (architecture, L0), K=5 occasions are already available per cell; this is exactly the regime where Lincoln-Petersen (K=2), Chao2 and jackknife are applicable but marginal. For K>5 we must train more seeds (the experiments repo should contain the training recipe; 200M samples at width 4,096 in a 768-dim synthetic world is cheap on one GPU, but I did not verify run time).
- **Truth is known per feature and ordered by frequency**, so the capture history against ground truth (oracle history) and the across-seed history are both computable; the p_i vector gives exact catchability covariates.
- **Built-in "uncatchable mass":** at width 4,096, features 4,096 to 16,383 are out-of-SAE. Their Zipf probabilities are by construction lower, so this is a tail-truncation experiment: does Chao estimate about 4,096 (recoverable under this architecture) or about 16,384 (total)? The full-width sweep (16,384 latents) gives the opposite regime where the SAE has enough latents but hedging/absorption still prevents recovery.
- **Pitfall:** the zoo was trained on the v1 world, whose children magnitudes are not scaled by the parent. Use the v1 world to evaluate the v1 SAEs (the model files are still `synth-sae-bench-16k-v1`), and v2 for any new training, or pin the generator version explicitly.
- **Dependence structure to expect:** the 128 hierarchy trees and the low-rank correlation make catches of siblings dependent (absorption and hedging), so a clean test for the "independent individuals" assumption is available: compare Chao on all 16k features vs on the non-hierarchical 5,504.

**Chanin (2026), "Are Sparse Autoencoder Benchmarks Reliable?", arXiv 2605.18229.** (HTML https://arxiv.org/html/2605.18229.) Further confirmed details: trains 35 SAEs at width 4,096 (5 architectures x 7 L0 values {15,...,45}) on SynthSAEBench-16k; diagnostic constructions "best-K", random-init, permuted-decoder, perfect-oracle SAEs; the real-LM reseed analysis re-runs each SAEBench evaluation n times with different seeds on the canonical Gemma Scope 65k SAE (Gemma-2-2B layer 12; five reseeds per a ResearchGate listing, unverified). **Important correction to D:** "reseed noise" there is evaluation noise for a fixed SAE, not variation between independently trained SAEs. Code: https://github.com/decoderesearch/saebench-reliability-audit (includes the permuted-decoder and random-init controls, directly useful as null catches).

### 2.2 Sharkey et al. and early dictionary-size work (priority 2)

**Sharkey, Braun, Millidge (2022), "[Interim research report] Taking features out of superposition with sparse autoencoders", LessWrong.** https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/z6QQJbtpkEAX3Aojj/interim-research-report-taking-features-out-of-superposition (mirror https://www.conjecture.dev/research/interim-research-report-taking-features-out-of-superposition-with-sparse-autoencoders).
- Seen: toy setup with 512 ground-truth features in 256 dims (learned dictionary J versus ground-truth G); recovery worked with learned dictionary roughly 1x to 8x the true count, MMCS declining only slowly beyond; MMCS compares learned vs ground-truth directions, and counts only the most similar elements when sizes differ; for the real LM (no ground truth) two learned dictionaries of different sizes are compared, with the smaller set as reference; stated goal: find the best dictionary size and L1 coefficient without ground truth using three proposed methods (which the explainer by AIZI says were hard to apply).
- **What went wrong (as far as seen):** (a) the "diagonal" structure in the original plot was an artefact of dividing the L1 coefficient by dictionary size (replication finding); (b) the technique "worked on simulated data but struggled on real models" (from the replication's framing); (c) cross-size agreement says features are consistent, not how many there are; MMCS rises quickly with size, then **plateaus**, so a plateau tells you a minimum size at which the core is captured, not a count of the long tail.
- **They did not infer the true number of features from MMCS between dictionary sizes in any statistical sense.** Our design (capture-recapture with explicit occasion structure and variance) is genuinely new relative to this.

**Replications (LessWrong/AF):** "[Replication] Conjecture's Sparse Coding in Toy Models" (https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/E8imxQo96WgDCMxkA/) and "...in Small Transformers" (https://www.alignmentforum.org/posts/vBcsAw4rvLsri3JAj/). Toy replication: code shared because the original is proprietary; additional replications by Trenton Bricken and Adam Shai are mentioned. Small-transformer replication (32 residual dims versus 16 in the original): max cosine similarity (MCS) distribution between dictionaries of different sizes is **highly bimodal** (a consistent core plus a tail of unmatched), and MMCS rises fast then plateaus; learned features are not sparse in the neuron basis. For us: the bimodality is the signature of heterogeneous catchability, and the fraction in the low mode is the "unmatched mass" that Lincoln-Petersen/Chao would try to extrapolate.

**Sharkey (2023), "Taking features out of superposition with SAEs more quickly with informed initialization"** (https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/YJpMgi7HJuHwXTkjk/). Seen: raw-data initialisation gives early MMCS above 0.6 but does not reach high MMCS faster than orthogonal init; reaching 0.99 MMCS requires recovering **rare features**, which may be absent from the sample or hidden behind common ones. Quotable support for the premise that the tail drives incompleteness.

**Anthropic Circuits Updates, May 2023** (https://transformer-circuits.pub/2023/may-update/index.html). Seen: synthetic test with sparse vectors randomly projected to 32 dims, dictionary learning (MOD with LASSO inner loop) over L1 coefficient and dictionary size; solutions "bounce" when dictionary size equals the true number of factors; the rate-distortion Pareto frontier is occupied by solutions with the correct number of factors, where the best MMCS is obtained; the authors say this "seems to be an effective tool for determining the correct number of features, at least for synthetic data". This is the only precedent for estimating N from dictionary learning outputs that I found. Test it as a comparator estimator in Phase 1 (rate-distortion knee vs capture-recapture) on synthetic data.

**Cunningham et al. (2023), arXiv 2309.08600** (Cunningham, Ewart, Riggs, Huben, Sharkey; ICLR 2024). The searches returned only the abstract and summaries. No toy-model, MMCS or count-estimation results were seen. The paper's contribution is the interpretability of features, not a count. Do not cite it for counting.

**Other items surfaced:**
- Robert AIZI, "Research Report: Sparse Autoencoders find only 9/180 board state features in OthelloGPT" (https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/BduCMgmjJnCtc7jKc/): 9 of 180 probe-findable board features classified with high accuracy by SAE latents; edit raises to 33/180 (>=0.9 AUROC) with earlier layers and untied weights; commenters report up to about 30 on layer 3. Dictionary size not seen. Shows the fraction found is low in a real model with known truth, and sensitive to layer and architecture.

### 2.3 Bricken et al. 2023 and Templeton et al. 2024 (priority 3)

**Bricken et al. (2023), "Towards Monosemanticity" (Anthropic; https://transformer-circuits.pub/2023/monosemantic-features/index.html).** Search coverage was only secondary. Seen: a 512-neuron MLP layer of a one-layer transformer decomposed into more than 4,000 features (main run 4,096 = 8x); dictionaries of different sizes studied (secondary: 512 up to 131,072, **unverified**); feature splitting = as the dictionary grows, one feature splits into several (the "dog" to "corgi/poodle" style explanation is a blog paraphrase); universality = similar features found across different seeds and across different models (an Arabic-script feature is the secondary example); interpretability scored by correlation (a summary says Spearman on 60 examples, unverified). Models are said to implement "up to 100x or more features than neurons" (blog paraphrase, not a verified quote). **The numeric universality correlations (e.g. A/1) were not retrievable.** Use only the qualitative statement in the paper, and quote numbers only after reading the primary page.

**Templeton et al. (2024), "Scaling Monosemanticity" (https://transformer-circuits.pub/2024/scaling-monosemanticity/).** Seen in secondary summaries: the authors state that their feature suite is incomplete and that the 34M SAE gives evidence that the features found are an incomplete description of the model's internal representations; the probability of finding a feature for a concept correlates with the concept's frequency in training data (rarer concepts are much less likely to have a dedicated feature); London-boroughs example: only about 60% of boroughs have a feature in the 34M SAE (secondary summary of the paper's coverage analysis, unverified); scaling feature exploration is stated to be an open problem. **I could not confirm any quoted estimate of how many features exist or of the compute needed to find all features** (my earlier recollection in D stays unverified; do not cite it). An arXiv listing 2605.29358 carries the same title; it appeared in multiple results (abs, html, pdf, alphaxiv, and a Pith AI-generated review), so it is an arXiv posting of the same work, but cite the transformer-circuits.pub URL as primary.
- For us: the "coverage vs concept frequency" analysis is a field precedent for a **frequency-stratified catch probability**. Capture-recapture with frequency covariates (Huggins) is the statistical generalisation of that analysis.

### 2.4 Toy models, board games, InterpBench, random transformers (priority 4)

- **Elhage et al. (2022), "Toy Models of Superposition" (https://transformer-circuits.pub/2022/toy_model/, arXiv 2209.10652).** Verified in outline: ReLU toy model, input features with importance weights and sparsity, projection to a smaller hidden space, phase change between dedicated, superposed and unrepresented features, uniform-polytope geometry, link to adversarial examples. The specific parameters I proposed to check (n=20, m=5, importance 0.7^i) were **not confirmed**. Relevance: the "unrepresented" regime (features dropped because dimension is insufficient) is the analogue of structurally uncatchable features; the phase diagram says which features are catchable depends on importance x sparsity. Use as the simplest generator arm (no hierarchy, no correlation) for sanity checks.
- **Karvonen et al. (2024), "Measuring Progress in Dictionary Learning for Language Model Interpretability with Board Game Models", arXiv 2408.00113** (NeurIPS 2024; Karvonen, Wright, Rager, Angell, Brinkmann, Smith, Verdun, Bau, Marks). Confirmed: chess and Othello transformers; SAEs on residual stream after layer 6; supervised metrics from transcripts (board reconstruction, **coverage** = mean over researcher-specified board-state properties of max F1 over latents); p-annealing training; more than 500 SAEs per game released. Coverage table: trained SAE 0.48 (chess), 0.52 (Othello); random-GPT baseline 0.11 and 0.27; linear probe 0.98 and 0.99. Code `github.com/adamkarvonen/SAE_BoardGameEval`; SAEs `adamkarvonen/chess_saes`, `adamkarvonen/othello_saes` on HF. Relevance: **a real-model testbed with a known (researcher-specified) feature list**, where capture history against the list is computable; coverage can serve as the "truth" for the fraction found. The 500+ SAEs vary in architecture/size/sparsity (seed structure unverified). Caveat: the feature list is not guaranteed complete, so it is a lower bound on population (a "known-subpopulation" design).
- **Gupta et al. (2024), "InterpBench: Semi-Synthetic Transformers for Evaluating Mechanistic Interpretability Techniques", arXiv 2407.14494** (NeurIPS 2024 Datasets and Benchmarks). Confirmed: transformers trained with Strict IIT (SIIT) to implement known circuits; motivation: Tracr models are sparse and have no superposition. It provides **circuit** ground truth, not SAE-feature ground truth. Relevance for Phase 3 (circuit-level capture-recapture on planted circuits), not Phase 1.
- **Heap, Lawson, Farnik, Aitchison (2025), "Sparse Autoencoders Can Interpret Randomly Initialized Transformers", arXiv 2501.17727** (v2 Jan 2026). Confirmed: SAE latents from random and trained transformers are about equally interpretable by an open-source autointerp pipeline; reconstruction/explained-variance metrics are comparable (secondary); features from random models look simpler (secondary hypothesis tied to token activation distributions). Relevance: **negative-control design.** SAEs trained on random-transformer activations give a population of "features" with no learned computation; the recapture rate of those across seeds is a null for "reproducible but meaningless" features. Also warns that autointerp-based "interpretable" is not a good identity criterion. Related: Korznikov et al. (2602.14111) frozen-random baselines (cited in D) cite this line of work.

### 2.5 Identifiability theory (priority 5)

- **Cui, Zhang, Wang, Wang (2025/2026), "On the Limits of Sparse Autoencoders: A Theoretical Framework and Reweighted Remedy", arXiv 2506.15963** (ICLR 2026; v1 title "On the Theoretical Understanding of Identifiable Sparse Autoencoders and Beyond"). https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.15963. Confirmed: first closed-form analysis of SAE optimum under the superposition hypothesis; SAEs generally fail to fully recover ground-truth monosemantic features unless the ground-truth features are extremely sparse; sufficient conditions for identifiability: extremely sparse ground-truth features, sparse SAE activation, enough SAE hidden dimensions; standard SAEs show **feature shrinking** (recovered features smaller in magnitude) and **feature vanishing** (some features disappear); proposed fix WSAE (reweights dimensions by degree of polysemanticity). No quantitative recovered fraction was seen. Implication: a fraction of features is deterministically unrecoverable for a given (data, SAE) pair, and it correlates with feature sparsity (note: the theory conditions on per-sample co-activation sparsity, not on how often a single feature fires, so it does not contradict the empirical finding that rare features are missed; this reading is mine, not the paper's).
- **Tang, Saini et al. (2025), "A Unified Theory of Sparse Dictionary Learning in Mechanistic Interpretability: Piecewise Biconvexity and Spurious Minima", arXiv 2512.05534** (Yiming Tang, Harshvardhan Saini et al.; first author per the search summary; arXiv title as seen). SAEs, transcoders, crosscoders cast as piecewise biconvex optimisation; maps global solutions, sources of non-identifiability, and spurious local minima that yield polysemantic features; offered as explanation for absorption and dead neurons; suggests "feature anchoring" to restore identifiability. A Pith review notes the derivation omits non-linear activations. Implication: **spurious local minima mean seeds can fall into different basins, which is the mechanism of seed-dependent catches** and is not a random-sample story.
- **Taming Polysemanticity in LLMs: Provable Feature Recovery via SAEs, arXiv 2506.14002.** Confirmed: statistical model where polysemantic features are sparse mixtures of monosemantic concepts; new notion of identifiability (epsilon-identifiability under sparsity and balance conditions); algorithm Group Bias Adaptation (GBA) adjusts biases so that neuron groups target different activation frequencies; proved recovery of all monosemantic features under the model; empirical results on LLMs up to 1.5B (Qwen2.5-1.5B). No recovery fraction in the results. Relevance: "feature balance" conditions are about relative frequencies of features, so frequency heterogeneity is itself an identifiability condition.
- **Classical dictionary learning:** the search returned the standard references but not their theorems. Seen: Spielman-Wang-Wright (complete dictionaries, LP-based recovery for Bernoulli-Gaussian coefficients; the sparsity bound is believed to be on the order of sqrt(n) per column, but this was NOT confirmed, arXiv 1206.5882); Arora-Ge-Moitra and Agarwal et al. (overcomplete incoherent dictionaries, initialisation + alternating minimisation with sample counts polynomial in the dictionary size; arXiv 1308.6273 for Arora et al., unverified ID); Gribonval-Schnass (local identifiability, 2010) with overcomplete extension in Schnass 2015 (arXiv 1401.6354, "Local Identification of Overcomplete Dictionaries"); Wu and Yu (2018) (arXiv 1505.04363, sufficient and almost necessary condition for local identifiability of l1 dictionary learning); Jung et al. "Performance Limits of Dictionary Learning for Sparse Coding" (arXiv 1402.4078); Vainsencher et al. "Sample Complexity of Dictionary Learning" (arXiv 1312.3790). Seen claim: for the square case N proportional to p log p samples suffice for local identifiability; for the general overcomplete case sample requirements can be exponential in the dimension. **Take-away for the project:** classical guarantees are for fixed K=#atoms with homogeneous Bernoulli sparsity and incoherent atoms. SAE regimes (heavy-tailed atom frequencies, N >> d, correlated atoms) violate these, so "what fraction is recoverable" has no clean theoretical answer. It must be measured by simulation.

**Conclusion:** for real LMs we must train our own seeds. The only ready-made replicates are synthetic.

### 2.7 Feature matching methods (priority 7)

- **PW-MCC (Song et al., 2505.20254):** as in D. Hungarian on |cosine| of decoder columns; reaches about 0.80 for TopK SAEs on LLM activations in the best design; tracks ground truth in synthetic settings.
- **SAE Match (Balagansky et al., 2410.07656):** data-free matching via linear assignment after folding JumpReLU thresholds into weights; as in D. Ties to 2606.12138 (same group).
- **Semantic optimal transport (Cao, Do, Thai; 2605.28567, submitted 27 May 2026):** represents each feature as an activation-weighted distribution over the hidden states that express it, projected to a shared reference space, compared with Wasserstein distance; designed for cross-layer matching and for grouping circuit features into "supernodes"; claims invariance to activation rescaling, stability to perturbations, recovery of true matches under finite-sample margin conditions, and outperforming decoder-vector and LLM-judge baselines (no numbers seen). For us: a candidate second matching rule that goes beyond decoder cosine (distinguishes features with similar decoder direction but different activation contexts; helps with hedging/absorption where cosine is misleading). Also notes that cross-SAE feature alignment is weak (cites the 30% figure).
- **"SAEs Are a Shared Coordinate System for Symbolic Matching" (Yang, Meng, Zhang, Han; 2610.19636):** SAE-IDF: encode each symbol in its native context into a sparse code (Gemma Scope / Llama Scope 131k features, Gemma-2-9B layer 20 or Llama-3.1-8B layer 15), re-weight dimensions by rarity (IDF), match by cosine; 12 tasks over 6 symbolic system types, beats the dense-embedding baseline by up to 45 points; code `github.com/AI-Interpretability-Illinois/Symbolic-Merge`. **Not about matching features between SAE runs.** What to borrow: rarity weighting as a way to emphasise informative features when comparing runs; and a reminder that SAE codes give a shared basis only if the SAE is shared.
- **Lan et al. (2410.06981):** activation-correlation matching across different models (from D).

Matching is the largest design risk in the proposal and all of these methods have only been validated by downstream task performance, not by identification of ground-truth matches. In a synthetic world we can score any matcher by whether matched pairs correspond to the same true feature: this is a publishable sub-result (matcher accuracy as a function of threshold, width and architecture).

### 2.8 Feature-frequency distributions in real SAEs (priority 8)

- **Central-region power law** (Empirical Evaluation of Progressive Coding for SAEs, arXiv 2505.00190): TopK SAEs of 65k, 32k, 16k latents, evaluated on 10^5 unseen tokens; activation-frequency exponent -1.00 to -1.20 (R^2 0.868 to 0.887) over the central region; the tail drops faster than the power law; squared-activation analog exponent -1.03 to -1.27. Interpretation in the paper: the least-used features fire even more rarely than a power law predicts. Caveat: the exponent is for frequency vs rank *within a fixed-width SAE* and is censored by the SAE's own width and dead-latent handling, so it is not the frequency law of the underlying true features (selection effect).
- **Log-normal densities** (Gao et al., 2406.04093): log of latent density is approximately Gaussian; no multi-modal density observed (unlike Bricken et al. 2023); activation distributions of single latents can be multi-modal in early layers. Dead latents defined as not firing in about 10^7 tokens; 7% dead in the 16M TopK case (from D).
- **Practitioner rule** (AF post on GPT-2 small SAEs): healthy log10 sparsity histogram sits between roughly -5 and -3; dense features are above 1/100 frequency.
- **Dense latents** (Sun et al., 2506.15679, NeurIPS 2025): dense latents (fire on 10 to 50% of tokens in Gemma-2 residual SAEs; secondary figure) often come in antipodal pairs (decoder cosine near -1); ablating the dense subspace reduces dense latents in retrained SAEs, so dense latents are a property of the residual stream, not a training artefact. Gao et al. had conjectured they are optimisation failures. For us: dense latents and their antipodal duplicates are "easy catches" (catch probability near 1, with duplicate matches inside a run), and must be dealt with explicitly (deduplicate antipodal pairs, treat as separate stratum).
- **Quantization model** (Michaud et al., from D): frequency index about 1+alpha with alpha about 0.08, i.e. much flatter than the -1.0 to -1.2 central exponent of SAE latent frequencies. These are different objects (quanta of skills vs SAE latent firing rates). Do not mix them: they bracket plausible values.
- Also seen but unread: "Understanding sparse autoencoder scaling in the presence of feature manifolds" (2509.02565), which assumes feature frequencies decay as a power law with exponent (1+alpha) and says SAEs learn features roughly in order of concept frequency.

**Parameterisation guidance for simulation (inference, not from a single source):** use Zipf rank-frequency p_i = c * i^-a with a in {0.8, 1.0, 1.2, 1.5} for the *true* feature spectrum, plus a log-normal arm (sigma of log10-density about 0.5 to 1; this is a guess calibrated to "mostly between -5 and -3") and a truncated-tail arm (extra decay beyond rank R) to match the observed sharp tail decline; always add a floor at p_min so that the expected number of firings in the training sample is at least a few.

## 3. Leads, ideas and experiments (updated)

1. **Phase 1a, zero-cost pilot on the released zoo.** Download `synth-sae-bench-16k-v1-saes`. For each (architecture, L0) cell, K=5 seeds: (i) compute ground-truth capture histories (Hungarian GT-MCC matching with a threshold sweep 0.5 to 0.95, plus latent-firing F1 matching); (ii) compute across-seed capture histories using decoder-cosine Hungarian matching between seed pairs and connected components; (iii) run Lincoln-Petersen/Chapman (pairs), Chao2, jackknife-1/2 on both. Targets: N_rec (features caught by at least one seed or by an oracle super-union), N_in (4,096), N_all (16,384). Cheap and answers whether estimators target the right quantity.
2. **Phase 1b, vary what the zoo cannot.** Train additional seeds (K up to 20) at widths {1,024; 4,096; 16,384} and with independent data streams versus shared data order (the zoo's seed protocol should be read from the experiments repo: **check whether seeds change init only, data order only, or both**). Use v2 world for new runs.
3. **World modifications via the SAELens generator** (parameters to expose: alpha, p_min, p_max, magnitude mu/sigma, hierarchy on/off, correlation rank/scale): factorial of {Zipf alpha} x {hierarchy on/off} x {correlation on/off}. Hierarchy-off/correlation-off recovers the independent-individuals baseline; hierarchy-on shows the cost of absorption in the estimator.
4. **Decomposition of error sources:** estimates from oracle histories (no matching error) versus matched histories (adds matching error) versus true N: this separates heterogeneity bias from identity noise, and gives matcher accuracy curves. Use the permuted-decoder and random-init SAEs in `saebench-reliability-audit` as null recapture controls (calibrate the false-match rate of the threshold).
5. **Rate-distortion-knee comparator** (Circuits Update, May 2023): fit the same synthetic worlds with a width sweep and compare the knee estimate of N with the capture-recapture estimate; both are failure-prone under heavy tails, so compare them in the Zipf-alpha sweep.
6. **Frequency-stratified analysis** (Templeton): estimate capture probability as a function of firing probability using the known p_i in synthetic worlds, then check whether Huggins with log-frequency as covariate recovers the true logistic slope; then plug in real SAE frequency covariates (decoder-side/encoder-side firing rates) in Phase 2.
7. **Known-subpopulation design on real models:** Karvonen board-game SAEs (500+ per game; coverage 0.48/0.52) and OthelloGPT probes (180 probe features): treat the supervised feature list as an "known subpopulation": mark-recapture style (use supervised features as marks, estimate catch rate of unmarked ones via the ratio), a natural Phase 2 validation where truth is partly known. Caveat: researcher-specified lists are incomplete.
8. **Random-transformer null (Heap et al.):** train K seeds of SAEs on a random transformer; the cross-seed recapture of random-model features gives the baseline recapture of non-meaningful structure; compare to the trained model's rates to separate "reproducible because of the data" from "reproducible because of the model's learned computation".
9. **Matcher bake-off in the synthetic world:** decoder-cosine Hungarian, strict (encoder+decoder agreement), activation-correlation (Lan), semantic OT (2605.28567), mutual-NN; score each by matching precision/recall against true feature identity. The winner in synthetic data gets used in Phase 2.
10. **Union-of-seeds accumulation curve:** number of distinct matched features vs K (K=1 to 20); compare to what Chao2/jackknife extrapolate from K=5; the accumulation curve in a synthetic world with known N_rec gives a direct truth check of the extrapolation.
11. **Dense/antipodal handling:** deduplicate antipodal decoder pairs (cos about -1) before matching; track dense latents as a separate stratum with catch probability near 1.

## 4. Pitfalls and warnings (new or sharpened)

1. **Seed zoo provenance:** v1 SAEs were trained on a world with `scale_children_by_parent=False`, mismatching the paper; v2 world differs only in that flag. Pin the world version to the SAE version, and note that the paper's reported numbers may correspond to the True setting (unclear which setting the SAE zoo's stats were computed under).
2. **Reseed in Chanin's audit means evaluation reseeding of one fixed SAE.** Do not cite it as a measure of cross-training variability.
3. **Non-independence is mechanistic, not only procedural:** spurious local minima and piecewise-biconvex non-identifiability (2512.05534), plus extreme-sparsity conditions (2506.15963), mean the "unrecovered" set is partly deterministic given data and architecture; seeds explore different basins of the same landscape. Chao assumes individuals have fixed capture probabilities; here the heterogeneity is partly structural (features with p_i too small are never caught at any seed), so the estimator targets the "catchable" population only.
4. **Dependence built into the generator:** with correlation, feature firing indicators are dependent, and with hierarchy, siblings are mutually exclusive; both make catches of related features dependent. The estimator's variance assumptions (independent individuals) are violated by design in SynthSAEBench; report coverage of intervals with hierarchy on/off.
5. **MMCS plateau is not a count:** Sharkey et al.'s curves plateau at a minimum dictionary size; do not present plateau as N. The "bounce" heuristic is validated only on toy data with equal-frequency factors.
6. **Frequency-law exponent applies to SAE latents, not true features** (selection by the SAE), and its tail falls faster than a pure power law. Using -1.0 to -1.2 directly as the truth spectrum will under-state the long-tail richness if the SAE truncates; also Michaud's near-1.08 index describes quanta, not latents.
7. **Do not cite numbers that I could not confirm:** Templeton's "how many features exist" estimate; Bricken's universality correlations; SynthSAEBench alpha/p_min/p_max/mean L0; Gerasimov's stable fraction and threshold; Ghosh's strict-match definition; Paulo-Belrose code location.
8. **2610.19636 is mis-scoped in the brief** if read as an SAE matching method; it is symbolic matching through SAE codes.
9. **Coverage on real models is bounded by the researcher list** (Karvonen coverage, OthelloGPT probes), so "found fraction" there is a lower bound on the catchable population.

## 6. Updated Phase 1 recommendations

1. **Use SynthSAEBench-16k (v2 world, v1 SAE zoo for the pilot) as the primary Phase 1 testbed.** Start with the released 5-seed zoo to get the whole pipeline (matching, histories, estimators, truth) running within days, then train more seeds with our own settings.
2. **Make the estimand explicit and three-fold:** N_all=16,384; N_in=4,096 (width-limited); N_rec(arch, L0, tokens) = features recovered above a GT-MCC threshold by an oracle super-union of many seeds. Evaluate each estimator against all three. Expect estimates near N_rec if anything.
3. **K matters:** with K=5 only Chao2/jackknife/Lincoln-Petersen are feasible; plan K=10 to 20 for log-linear and Huggins with covariates. Training a seed is cheap in this world (200M synthetic samples, 768 dims).
4. **Always compute two histories** (ground-truth and cross-seed) and a matcher-accuracy table; run the matcher bake-off (cosine Hungarian, strict, activation-correlation, semantic OT).
5. **Parameterise the heavy tail with a sweep, not a single value:** Zipf alpha in {0.8, 1.0, 1.2, 1.5}; add a log-normal arm and a faster-than-power-law truncation arm (observed in real SAEs). The SynthSAEBench default alpha/p_min/p_max must be read from the SAELens code or paper appendix before we reuse it; if unavailable, fit alpha from the per-feature firing rates of the generator directly (samples are cheap).
6. **Turn off the structure first:** run hierarchy-off and correlation-off worlds to validate the estimators in the independent-individuals regime, then switch each on to measure degradation (absorption/hedging dependence); this gives the cleanest "when do capture-recapture assumptions fail" result.
7. **Include controls:** permuted-decoder and random-init SAEs (null recapture), oracle SAE (perfect catches), dense/antipodal-duplicate handling, and an "injected ultra-rare feature" arm showing non-identifiability of the never-caught mass.
8. **Add comparators:** rate-distortion knee (Circuits Update 2023), accumulation curve of union vs K, and reconstruction-based heuristics (to show the loss is not informative, cf. Korznikov).
9. **Phase 2 data plan:** train our own seeds (Pythia-70M/160M or GPT-2 small; independent data order vs shared) since no real-LM replicate set is public; use Karvonen's board-game SAEs and OthelloGPT probe lists as a known-subpopulation validation; use Heap-style random-transformer SAEs as null; use Gemma Scope ladders only as nested, dependent occasions.
10. **Open items to resolve by reading primary text** (when WebFetch is available or via the repos): SynthSAEBench Section 3 and appendices (alpha, p_min, p_max, mean L0, mu/sigma), the zoo's seed protocol (init vs data order), Gerasimov's matching threshold and stable fraction, Ghosh's strict-match rule, Bricken's universality numbers.
